# Thesis (v1, 2026-10-05)

## What we offer

We offer a glass box. The Virelai solver is dropped into an ARC-AGI-3 world it has never seen — a world whose entire vocabulary is a 64×64 frame of 16 colours, whose action interface is a handful of keys and a click, whose goal is never stated — and probes it along a fixed sequence of cybernetic gates. It then emits a report in its own vocabulary: **what it is and can do in this world, what the world's mechanics are, and, when a win has been observed, why that win occurred in terms of the model it built — carried forward as a prediction of the next level's goal.** Every line of the report cites the transitions that support it; every claim it cannot support is declared unknown. The report, not the score, is the result. Accuracy is what we hope for; checkability is what we offer.

## The order of inquiry, and why order

Six questions, in this order. *Each step removes one possible cause of change before the next step reads the evidence.* Until you know what you did and what the clock did, you cannot tell what the world did in response. The claim is about the procedure — this order is sufficient to read a world of this kind — not about how any particular intelligence behaves.

| # | Question | Gate(s) | Status at `c9b246b` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Find yourself.** What do I control; what is the locus of my action; what are my limits? | G0 action surface, G1 ego | BUILT |
| 2 | **Find time.** What changes regardless of which action I take; what runs down; what ends a life? | G2 counter, life record | BUILT |
| 3 | **Find the response.** What responds to me, under what conditions, with what law? | G3 levers, G4 reactions / relations / orbits / law | BUILT |
| 4 | **Find the players.** What else acts or changes on its own? | — | **NOT BUILT.** The report declares it unknown. |
| 5 | **Model.** Assemble the certified products into one explicit world model, every claim with its evidence. | R9-R25 world report | IN BUILD |
| 6 | **Value.** When a win is observed: state what was good in the model's terms; lift it; freeze it at the next level's entry as the goal hypothesis; grade it by the next win. | win capsule, level lesson, LW-1–4 | PARTIAL (capsule and lesson built; lift, freeze, grade in build) |

Before a win, the only good the agent can know is epistemic and survival: uncertainty reduced, counter not expired, lives kept. After a win, good is the win condition. Pursuing the goal (planning) is outside this paper.

## Scope

Contents don't matter; form does. The six questions follow from the published rules of ARC-AGI-3 (frame-only vocabulary §2.3; Core Knowledge priors §3.4; a terminal frame and an unstated objective §2.1, §2.3; random play cannot beat non-tutorial levels §3.5; composition across levels §3.4; novelty across environments §3.4). Necessary archetypes (action surface, terminal transition, action-independent vs action-dependent change, responsive vs inert, persistence across levels and lives, history-dependence) are distinguished from contingent instances (a visible counter, an avatar); every contingent gate may close ABSENT. No world content enters the solver by construction. ARC is a toy world built to this specification; the submission is a toy model of reading it.

## The three claims

- **C1 · Theory / Universality.** The questions are deducible from the rules, not learned from games. An argument; no run required. This claim carries Universality, because the empirical record cannot.
- **C2 · Mechanism / Completeness.** From the frame alone, the solver emits a report in which every line cites a transition and every unknown is declared. Graded per claim — correct / wrong / unverifiable / missed / invented, by executor — against answer keys filed before any report is read, on 25 public worlds, 22 of which no gate was developed against.
- **C3 · Goal-setting, as prediction (Director's ruling, 2026-10-05).** From level N's observed win the solver states, at level N+1's entry and before any N+1 action, the goal hypothesis for N+1; the N+1 win grades it reconfirmed, specialised or killed; the pattern repeats N+1 → N+2 (canon: `SPEC_SOLVER_LOOPS_v2` §7, `GATE_THESES_v3` G8/G9). The vocabulary is every certified entity on the level — levers and non-levers alike — with their relations (position, extent, containment, adjacency, alignment, shape), keyed on entity type, never colour: the vc33 level-2 win cannot be stated in lever states alone (`LEARN_TO_WIN_ZOOM_v1_2026-10-05`). A first-win explanation whose configuration never occurred before is flagged *novelty-separated* and held weak until a second win supports it. Two evidence kinds, labelled: *replay-prospective* (generated from level ≤N records with the code blind to N+1; vc33 L1→L2) and *live-prospective* (frozen in a live run; vc33 L2→L3). Reported as a count of graded pairs. The thesis's own measurable signature, score-free: later levels collapse toward near-free deduction — the front-loaded action curve, prereg H0.

## What the paper does not claim

A competitive score. That gate order is necessary (only that it is sufficient; the ablation is future work). That a small model becomes a large one. That the agent plays as a human does. Generalisation to the private set (public ≠ private by ARC's design, §3.6). Goal pursuit.

## How it can be wrong, and who says so

Keys for all 25 public games are written by the Director and committed before any report is read; grading is by a non-author wherever possible and at least one game is graded blind. The report's own refusals and unknowns are counted, not hidden. Both vc33 wins to date were the controller's, not the model's. One run is one sample. The LLM is, until measured, a proposer whose proposals a code referee admits; its admission rate is the measurement of anything stronger.
