## 1 · The ruling, as chosen

> The controller works out lever relations itself from the recorded presses, with no Duck turn. Duck then confirms or extends them, with a set limit of turns per level, and the gate closes UNRESOLVED when the limit is hit, so it never loops.

## 2 · What it means

**G-1. The record answers first.** Every press on the level is a recorded transition, and the controller holds its exact diff: which lever regions changed, and how. From those transitions the controller certifies the relations G4-B asks for, with no Duck turn. Each relation cites the transitions it rests on.

**G-2. What counts as evidence.** An observed transition is evidence. Whether a prediction was made before it, and whether that prediction held, does not change what the frame shows.
- A contradicted prediction is dead, but the transition that killed it stays on the record as a fact.
- This keeps "evidence before belief". It removes only the requirement that evidence arrive through a supported prediction. That requirement caused 73 of the roughly 105 answer rejections in v7.

**G-3. Duck confirms or extends, on a budget.**
- Duck's G4-B and G4-C turns on one level share a fixed budget, counted in Duck turns. Rejected and cut-off turns count, because they cost the same time.
- A Duck answer may confirm the controller's relations, add a relation backed by recorded transitions, or freeze a prediction that tests one.

**G-4. When the budget is spent, the gate closes UNRESOLVED.**
- This is a declared end (axiom A2). The gate emits what is certified and lists what remains open.
- It never loops. A G4-C contradiction that would reopen G4-B after the budget is spent closes UNRESOLVED instead.
